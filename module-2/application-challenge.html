Application Challenge 2: Riverbend College Help-Desk Retrieval

Take the retrieval skill from HOS 2 to someone else's pipeline: measure how often it actually finds the answer, improve it with your own chunking, and decide when the bot should stop answering and send the student to a person.

Est. 60–90 minOutcome 2Module 2 · end of Week 4IndividualAI assistant allowed — disclose it5% of final grade

📏

Ground rules

Everything here runs in GitHub Codespaces on free tiers, with no credit card. Everything runs offline with no API key. Real Gemini embeddings are an optional comparison.
🏢

The scenario

Riverbend College (fictional) has an IT help-desk bot that answers student questions from six knowledge-base articles: passwords, MFA, Wi-Fi, printing, software, and Canvas. It's the same pipeline you reviewed in the HOS 2 lesson's warm-up, now runnable. It never crashes, and nobody knows how often it answers from the wrong passage.

You get the pipeline, the articles, and eval_set.json: 16 real student questions with the article and phrase that answers each one, plus 4 questions the articles can't answer.

  • The tests use an offline embedder (LocalEmbedder counts words) so everyone gets the same numbers. It's cruder than Gemini's, on purpose: some of its misses are ones real embeddings would fix, and telling those apart is part of the challenge.
📐

The spec

Every requirement below is checked by a provided test in tests/test_evaluate.py. Your code goes in evaluate.py.

FunctionMust do
hit_rate(eval_set, search_fn, k)Count a question as a hit when one of its top-k results has the right article and contains the answer phrase. Skip the unanswerable questions. Return the rate and the IDs of the misses. On the vendor's pipeline at k=1 it must report 0.5625.
chunk_article(article_id, text)Your chunking. Every chunk is labelled with its article, non-empty, and at most 800 characters (the chat model's prompt budget). No answer may be lost. It must beat the vendor's hit rate at k=1.
answer_or_abstain(question, search_fn, min_score)Look at the top result. Below min_score, or no result: abstain. Otherwise answer from it. Defaults to your MIN_SCORE.

The open decision: when to say "I don't know"

The help desk lead: "A wrong answer sends a student to the wrong office, or makes them miss a deadline. But 'I don't know' sends them to the ticket queue, and that's a two-day wait right now." Nobody set a threshold.

Choose MIN_SCORE. python run_eval.py prints a sweep showing, for each threshold, how many questions are answered right, answered wrong, and declined when they were answerable. There's no single right row. Prove your choice with your own tests and defend it in DECISIONS.md.

💡

Why the misses happen

In your memo, sort the misses (the vendor's and your remaining ones) by cause: chunking (the answer was cut off or diluted), vocabulary (the student's words aren't the article's words, like "Xbox" or "jammed"), or something else. Say which causes your chunking could fix, and which need something else.

🪜

Steps & what to submit

1Open it and run the tests

Open your own AC 2 repo (accept the AC 2 invite) in GitHub Codespaces. It installs everything for you. Then run:

pytest

Almost everything fails with NotImplementedError. That's your to-do list.

2Measure the vendor

Write hit_rate until test_vendor_baseline_on_the_real_questions passes. You may use an AI assistant; read what it writes, because you'll defend it.

3Chunk it your way

Write chunk_article. Try at least two strategies and keep the one that measures best:

python run_eval.py

Adding the article title to every chunk sounds helpful. Measure it before you assume.

4Make the threshold call

Write answer_or_abstain, set MIN_SCORE, and write at least two tests in tests/test_my_rules.py on the real questions: one the bot must still answer, one it must decline. The file shows how to build a real search_fn.

5Report and memo

Paste the output of python run_eval.py into REPORT.md and fill in DECISIONS.md. Optional: add your Gemini key to .env, run python run_eval.py --gemini, and note what changed.

Submit (via Brightspace)
  • evaluate.py and tests/test_my_rules.py
  • The output of pytest, pasted as text (all tests passing, including yours)
  • REPORT.md
  • DECISIONS.md
📊

How it's graded

Each criterion is graded on one part of your submission, at one of three levels.

CriterionWeightGraded onFullPartialMissing
Application10%REPORT.mdYour chunking runs on the help desk's articles and beats the vendor, with the misses listed.It runs, but the report is missing the sweep or the misses.It restates the HOS build, or doesn't run.
Analysis20%DECISIONS.md, section 2 (why the misses happen)Every miss is sorted by cause, with a specific question for each cause and what would fix it.Causes are named, but not tied to specific questions.No breakdown.
Evaluation30%DECISIONS.md, section 1A clear threshold, judged against stated criteria (which mistake is worse, for whom), using the sweep's numbers, with a threshold you rejected and why.A threshold with a reason, but no numbers or no rejected alternative.No rule, or no justification.
Design40%evaluate.py, your tests, and the pytest outputAll provided tests pass, and your own tests check your threshold on real questions, including the closest call.Most provided tests pass, or your own tests use only easy questions.It doesn't run, or you wrote no tests of your own.

How this differs from HOS 2's Understand & Refine: there you added a document to your own pipeline, by hand. Here the pipeline, the articles, and the decisions are someone else's, AI help is allowed, and half the grade (Analysis and Evaluation) is for the calls you make.

🧯

Common snags

  • test_vendor_baseline says 0.625 or 0.5. Check both conditions: the right source and the answer phrase in the text, ignoring upper/lower case. And skip questions whose source is None.
  • test_no_answer_is_lost fails. Your chunker dropped text, often the last piece of an article or the line right after a heading.
  • Your chunks score worse than you expected. With a word-count embedder, extra words in a chunk (titles, headings, overlap) dilute it. That's a finding worth a sentence in your memo, not a bug.
  • --gemini fails. It needs GEMINI_API_KEY in .env. It's optional; everything graded runs without it.
