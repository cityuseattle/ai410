Build a full-stack AI app, one module at a time

Ten weeks, five modules. In Modules 1–4 you build one app that gets smarter each time: an agent that calls tools, answers from your documents, survives bad model output, and runs on your phone. In Module 5 you combine all four into a project of your own, on your own or with a group.

Before Module 1: three questions for yourself

Two minutes, nothing to submit. Answer in your head or in a note, and look again at the end of the term.

  1. In one sentence, what is AI to you right now: something that gives answers, something you work with, or something else?
  2. Why might this course let you use AI in some stages and not in others?
  3. When an AI writes part of your code, what do you still need to be able to do yourself?

Many people start out treating AI as an answer machine. In this course it's a fast first draft that you test, explain and extend. Every AIC is built that way: it starts with AI and ends by hand. Step 4 shows the rule for each activity.

How each module runs: two weeks

Each module centres on an AIC (AI Challenge), worth 10% of your grade, in two parts: Part A: Build in Week 1 and Part B: Apply in Week 2, which ends with an individual challenge in a new scenario. Both parts go in one GitHub Classroom repo, and your last push before the end of Week 2 is your submission (there is no Brightspace upload for the AIC). It's graded once: 60 points for the build, 40 for the challenge. Every week also has a Concept Debate and a Knowledge Check, graded separately.

The AIC used to be called the Hands-On Skill (HOS). The GitHub Classroom repos keep the old name, so for AIC 1 you accept a repo called AI410-HOS01, and so on.
