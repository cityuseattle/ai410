What Weeks 9–10 are for

20% of courseOutcomes O1–O5Teams of three

The Term Project combines all 4 HOS — the agent and its tools, retrieval over your own documents, guardrails and background jobs, and a mobile client with push and observability — into one app a real user would want. You've built every piece once already. The work now is synthesis: deciding how the pieces fit, building the combined version, and showing it working live.

The project runs all term in three phases. By Week 9 your team has locked its scope (Phase 1) and has a rough working version (Phase 2), so Phase 3 starts with a short architecture review and a backlog, then builds. Week 10 finishes, freezes, rehearses, and presents.

Days 1–2: product backlog and Sprint 1

Scrum-liteKeep it brief

Write everything the project might need as user stories — "As a [user], I want [capability], so that [benefit]" — and tag each with the HOS it depends on. Then mark a small, realistic subset as Sprint 1: what you can actually finish in the rest of Week 9 and Week 10. Everything else stays backlog, not a promise. Your list stays in this browser; copy it into your PM tool when it's ready.

Depends on:

Coverage

Sprint 1 should touch at least two HOS end to end this week, and all four by the Week 10 demo.

Litmus test for each HOS

For every HOS, can you point to a specific part of your project and say why it's really that HOS? "This is HOS 2, and it's actually retrieval over documents users upload — not a hardcoded FAQ." If a HOS only has a token mention, the Architecture criterion and your final report will show it.

👥

Roles and a project management tool

Day 1–2

Teams of three (four with instructor approval): assign an owner per area, not per week. One workable split: agent & tools (HOS 1 + 3's guardrails), data & retrieval (HOS 2 + 3's worker), mobile & push (HOS 4 client), and observability & demo (HOS 4 traces, plus owning the demo script). With three people, one person takes two areas. Everyone still commits code: the Git Workflow & Collaboration criterion looks for commits from every member, not one big push before the deadline.

Build: same Create → Evaluate habit, bigger scope

Rest of Week 9 → Week 10
  • Start from your HOS 4 app. It already has all four layers. Change what the project needs; don't start over.
  • Scaffold with an AI assistant, then judge it. Use the Term Project Prompt Builder to scope what you ask it to build — the same way each HOS's Create/Scaffold stage worked.
  • Get one thin path working end to end first — for example, one question that retrieves from your documents and calls one tool — before widening any single layer.
  • Keep Evaluating on purpose. For each story, one test that should work and one that should fail gracefully. Your Evaluate habits from Weeks 1–8 are what keep demo day calm.
  • Rehearse in the simulators when you're unsure how a layer should behave: 1 · 2 · 3 · 4.

Standups

Async is fine

Brief, regular check-ins once building starts — a group chat is fine. The point is to catch "I've been stuck for three days" before demo week, not during it.

Demo plan — show every HOS working

Presentation · 20 pts

A suggested shape for the live-demo part of your 15-minute presentation (the rest is the problem, architecture and results on your 15 content slides). Adjust the times to your slot; keep the order.

~0:30The user and the problem. Who is this for, and what's hard for them today? One sentence each.
~3:00One live path through all 4 HOS. On the phone (HOS 4): ask a question answered from your documents (HOS 2) that triggers a tool (HOS 1); show a bad input handled by a guardrail (HOS 3); upload a document, keep chatting while it processes (HOS 3), and show the push arrive (HOS 4). Then open the trace (HOS 4) and point at retrieval, generation, and the score.
~0:45What didn't make it, and what you'd do next. Name the corner you cut — your Integration Debate synthesis in practice.
restQuestions. Expect "show me where HOS 2 is" — have the retrieval span ready.

Self-check and reflection

Feeds your Author Contribution statement

Self-check from the schedule: can you explain, without notes, how each of the 4 HOS shows up in your final project? Say it out loud once before the demo.

Reflection prompts

  • Each member: what you personally built (link commits or issues), one decision you argued for, and one thing a teammate did that made the project better.
  • Everyone: one moment where Evaluate caught an AI-generated mistake before it reached the demo.
